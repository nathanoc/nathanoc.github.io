A conditionally convergent series is one in which you can rearrange the terms such that it no longer converges. The [[Riemann series theorem]] implies that a series is conditionally convergent if and only if it is not absolutely convergent. This means that, taking the absolute value of each of the terms, the series diverges.

For example,
$$
1 - 1 + \frac12 - \frac12 + \frac13 - \frac13 + \cdots
$$
converges to zero. However, taking the absolute value of each term,
$$
1 + 1 + \frac12 + \frac12 + \frac13 + \frac13 + \cdots
$$
diverges (being twice the harmonic series). Therefore, this series is conditionally convergent.
