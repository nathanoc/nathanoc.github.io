The key point here is that the $E$ in $E=mc^2$ is the total energy of an object - i.e. both the rest energy and the kinetic energy.

When an object is at rest, $m$ is its rest mass.

When the object gains kinetic energy, $E$ increases - thus $m$ must also increase. So when an object is moving faster, it has more mass; when it is stationary, its mass is its rest mass. But for most purposes, this increase in mass is negligible - for an increase in energy of $1\pu{J}$, an object gains $\frac{1}{c^2}\pu{kg}$ of mass.
