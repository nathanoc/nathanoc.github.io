Notation: $\mathbf{a \cdot b}$

Maps two vectors onto a scalar

Given by sum of products of components ($a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_z$)

This is equivalent to $|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos \theta$ where $\theta$ is the angle between the vectors

Properties:
- Commutative
- Distributive - $\mathbf{a\cdot (b+c)}=a\mathbf{\cdot b + a \cdot c}$
- Can't be associative as the result is a scalar

If $\mathbf{a\cdot b}=0$ then $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are orthogonal, or one of their magnitudes is zero.

The [[Cosine rule\|cosine rule]] follows from taking $\mathbf{c=b-a}$ and squaring both sides using the scalar product.
